📋 Full race report

Setting

Round 4 took the WCT to the Mobility Resort Motegi in Japan. The 4.801 km Grand Prix course has 14 corners and shares its pit lane with the 2.4 km oval, where IndyCar raced from 1998 to 2011. Its character comes from two very hard stops, into Turn 1 and Turn 11, linked by a flowing middle section. It was dry, with the air at 21 °C and the track around 31 °C. The format was the usual one: open qualifying split into an AM and a Pro session, then a timed hour of racing — 33 laps — with one fuel stop.

Only twenty drivers started, 9 Pro and 11 AM, with a strength of field of 2268. The booth counted 35 to 38 registrations and regretted the many late cancellations. Yannick Wonnenberg was still ill. Andreas commentated alone on cas_sim_tv.

Qualifying

The AM session nearly went wrong for Andreas Bastian, who put the Porsche in the gravel on his out-lap. He recovered and kept improving to a 1:49.170, the fastest AM time and good enough for second on the combined grid. In the Pro session Mike Zocher, Dennis Richter and Klaus Oberländer queued first at pit exit. Maurice Becker found his time on his final lap, just as the chequered flag fell: 1:49.121, only 0.049 s quicker than Bastian. Becker said in the chat that the track had been six or seven degrees warmer for the Pro session. Richter (1:49.290), Marius Becker (1:49.307), Zocher (1:49.311) and Elias Schleich (1:49.356) completed the top six. The last corner cost many drivers their best lap.

Race — the opening phase

Becker kept the lead from pole. Bastian lost places off the line, and Mike Zocher, the Season 13 champion, went up the inside of his Speed Monkeys stablemate Richter to take second. After five laps Becker led Zocher by 2.8 s, with Richter third. At the back there was contact at the start involving Torsten Folkers. The booth thought the other car was Matthias Kleinjohann’s but was not sure, and this report does not assign it. Kleinjohann spun around minute 12 with rear damage and pitted early, on lap 14. Florian Roessler was caught up in the start and fell back to 17th.

Further back the early laps belonged to Michael Endres, who gained six places in the AM Aston Martin and ran side by side with Markus Heinzl, which the booth called fair. He then pitted on lap 9, dropped to last and disconnected after eight laps. The booth found no incident on the replay to explain it.

Race — Zocher’s chase and the black flag

For the first half the race had a real contest at the front. Zocher held Becker at around four seconds from lap 5 to lap 19 and set the fastest lap of the race, a 1:49.382, on lap 18. Richter ran a lonely third about five seconds further back. Then, coming in for his stop, Zocher was shown the black flag for speeding at pit entry. He served it together with his stop — 81 s stationary instead of the usual 39 — and rejoined 14th. He fought back to 11th. Becker said afterwards:

„Okay, ja ich muss auch sagen, dass Mike Zocher auch relativ gut war. … der hat mich jetzt so ein bisschen schwitzen gebracht, wo er noch so drei, vier Sekunden hinter mir war. Aber irgendwie hat er in der Boxengasse dann Mist gebaut.“

Race — the stops

The main stop window ran from lap 18 to lap 21, with Björn Krumpschmied first in. Stationary times were close, between 38.3 and 42.8 s; Andre Brechmann was quickest, and Becker and Gregor Micewski took 38.7 s. When Marius Becker stopped, Schleich finally got ahead of him after more than twenty laps within 1.5 s. Thomas Kuebler stayed out longer than the AM leaders and briefly led the class, then went off at pit entry; his 49.3 s stop dropped him from third to fifth in class. Dirk Bolte lost the car on a kerb shortly after his stop and hit the barrier, which cost him a second visit to the pits. Becker came out of the cycle with a 15.8 s lead and extended it to 18.6 s by the flag.

Race — the fight for second

With Zocher gone, second was between Elias Schleich and Dennis Richter. The two swapped the place in the stop phase, were side by side at times on the run to Turn 5, and in all spent twelve laps within 1.5 s of each other. Schleich held on, and Richter came back to two tenths in the closing laps but finished 0.39 s behind. Marius Becker ran wide late on and was passed by Bastian and by Gregor Micewski, who finished fourth from ninth on the grid. In the final laps Roessler passed Heinzl after a flash of the headlights and Alex Turek followed him through. On the last lap Klaus Oberländer had a big moment in the final corners; the booth first linked it to his fight with Marius Becker, then said it had nothing to do with him. He kept seventh.

AM — Bastian takes the lead

Andreas Bastian spent the race in the top six overall, at times ahead of the Pro cars, and won AM in fifth overall. Manfred Baar was second in class in eighth and Florian Roessler third in tenth after his recovery from the start. Turek, Heinzl and Kuebler completed the AM top six. The result moves Bastian to the top of the AM championship.

Notable incidents

  • Mike Zocher — black flag for speeding at pit entry while running second, served with his stop (81 s). From second to 14th; he finished 11th and kept the fastest lap.
  • Benjamin Warnow — went hard into the tyre barrier, spent 181 s in the pits for repairs and later disconnected after 22 laps (DNF).
  • Michael Endres — disconnected after eight laps; CLS scores him DSQ, below the minimum distance.
  • Thomas Kuebler — off at pit entry, a 49.3 s stop.
  • Dirk Bolte — lost the car on a kerb after his stop and hit the barrier.
  • Start contact involving Torsten Folkers; the other car was not clearly identified on air.

Post-race

Becker and Richter came to the interview room. Respect for the runner-up came first: „Ja, also Respekt an Elias. Das war wirklich brutal schnell gut.“ Andreas agreed that Schleich belongs in Pro: „Definitiv.“ Becker on his car and the track: „Ja, der Porsche geht schon ganz gut hier.“ and „Also ich mag die Strecke an sich ganz gerne.“ Richter looked back at his lap-1 spin at Zandvoort without excuses: „Ja, aber dann war ich zu hitzköpfig.“

Championship after Round 4

The Pro table has a new pair at the top. Maurice Becker and Gregor Micewski are level on 118, with Becker ahead in CLS; Becker has scored his after only three starts. Klaus Oberländer is third on 110 and Elias Schleich fourth on 106. Leon Klein, the leader after Zandvoort, did not start and drops to seventh. In AM, Andreas Bastian leads on 144 from Florian Roessler (140) and Thomas Kuebler (138). Teams: Speed Monkeys II 173, REVORYX eSports 161, NEON Simsports Blue 152. Full tables below.

Mobility Resort Motegi (Grand Prix)

2D telemetry replay — the 60-minute race compressed to 90 seconds. The circuit map is the real Motegi Grand Prix course (4.801 km), which shares its pit lane with the oval.

By the Numbers

Race Winner

Maurice Becker — lights to flag

Pole, all 33 laps led and the win — his second lights-to-flag victory in a row after Zandvoort. Only Mike Zocher stayed within reach, about 4 s back, until his penalty.

⏱️ Winning Margin

18.640 seconds

He was 2.8 s ahead after five laps, 3.8 s by lap 12 and 4.1 s before the stops. With Zocher out of the way he led by 15.8 s after the pit cycle and 18.6 s at the flag.

🕑 Qualifying

Becker on pole, 1:49.121

Two sessions, AM first, then Pro. Becker took pole on his last lap, just 0.049 s ahead of AM driver Andreas Bastian, who had been stuck in the gravel on his out-lap. Richter, Marius Becker and Zocher completed the top five.

🏆 Driver of the Day

Gregor Micewski — 0.835

#08 (NEON Simsports Purple, Pro) went from ninth to fourth with seven overtakes on three incident points. He is now level on 118 points with Becker at the top of the Pro championship.

Class Wins

Maurice Becker in Pro, Bastian in AM

Andreas Bastian won AM in fifth overall, ahead of Manfred Baar and Florian Roessler, and takes the AM championship lead. In Pro, Elias Schleich finished second ahead of Dennis Richter by 0.39 s.

🏴 Black Flag

Zocher: from second to 11th

Mike Zocher ran second for 18 laps and set the fastest lap, 1:49.382. A black flag for speeding at pit entry, served with his stop (81 s stationary instead of about 39), dropped him to 14th; he recovered to 11th.

⛽ Pit Window

Laps 18–21, 38.3–42.8 s

Most of the field made its fuel stop on laps 18–21; Kleinjohann came in early on lap 14. Brechmann was quickest at 38.3 s, Becker and Micewski took 38.7 s. Kuebler went off at pit entry (49.3 s). Folkers and Bolte stopped twice, and Warnow needed 181 s of repairs before he disconnected.

Conditions & Field

Dry, 21 °C, 20 starters

Dry, air 21 °C, no cautions. There were only 20 starters — 9 Pro, 11 AM — with a strength of field of 2268. Benjamin Warnow (lap 22) and Michael Endres (lap 8) dropped out after disconnecting.

Race Highlights

Maurice Becker repeated his Zandvoort performance at Motegi. He took pole by 0.049 s on his final qualifying lap, led every one of the 33 laps and won by 18.640 s. For the first half of the race Mike Zocher was the only driver within reach, holding second about four seconds back and setting the fastest lap. Then he was black-flagged for speeding at pit entry, served the penalty with his stop and fell to 14th. Becker said afterwards that Zocher had made him sweat until his pit-lane mistake.

That handed second to a long fight between Elias Schleich and Dennis Richter. Schleich, who had spent 21 laps within 1.5 s of Marius Becker, got ahead in the pit cycle and held off Richter to the flag by 0.39 s. Gregor Micewski came from ninth to fourth and was Driver of the Day. In AM, front-row starter Andreas Bastian won the class in fifth overall and took the AM championship lead. Andre Brechmann made the most passes of the night, from 15th to ninth.
