CAS GT3 WCT · Season 13 · Round 9

Indianapolis Motor Speedway (Road Course)

18 August 2026 · 28 starters · ~60-minute timed race (42 laps) · overcast and hazy, track 25.8 °C · Full race report ↗

2D telemetry replay — the 62-minute race compressed to 90 seconds. The circuit map is the real Indianapolis road course (3.92 km, 14 corners, part of it the oval).

By the Numbers

Race Winner

Yannick Wonnenberg

Pole, the lead into Turn 1 and 42 laps out of 42 in front — no lead change all race. His second win in a row, and in only his fifth start of Season 13.

⏱️ Winning Margin

1.730 seconds

Maurice Becker closed from four seconds to three and no further. Wonnenberg had held the gap deliberately and eased off completely for the last three laps — which is where the final margin came from.

Pole & Fastest Lap

1:23.672 / 1:24.040

The top five in qualifying covered 0.154 s, and Wonnenberg and Lukas Zörlaut were split by a single thousandth before the final runs. Maurice Becker took the race’s fastest lap on lap 19. Nobody matched a qualifying 1:23 in the race.

💥 The Race Behind

Becker vs. Richter, 20 minutes

Same move every lap — tow, outside into Turn 1, better exit line for the Ferrari. One pass, one immediate counter-pass, and only then a break. It cost them both far more than it cost the leader.

🏆 Driver of the Day

Robert Zellner

#360 (AM, McLaren) takes it on 0.772: seven places gained from P27 to P20, 16 overtakes, two incident points — and a mid-race crash recovered from along the way.

Class Wins

Warnow by 0.334 s

Ricardo Still led AM almost all race and threw it away with a late slide. Benjamin Warnow and Christoph Kiesel fought it out to the final corner. GDC went to Björn Krumpschmied, from Bernhard Wlach and Markus Heinzl.

Cleanest Run

Christoph Kiesel — zero

The only driver in the field to finish on 0 incident points, and he did it while fighting for the AM win. Toughest race: Kevin Homburg on 15, a black flag and 31 laps.

Conditions & Attrition

25.8 °C, hazy, no cautions

An overcast in-sim morning that never cleared, and a track everyone described as unusually slippery. 28 drivers appeared after a long summer withdrawal list; all 28 were classified and 22 finished on the lead lap, with not one caution period.

F1-style position track. The flat line at the top is the entire story of the lead. The interesting movement is Markus Groß dropping fourteen places in the opening laps and climbing back to P7 on the early stop, and the steady rise of Robert Zellner and Mike Girenz out of the last rows.

Incidents on Track

Every logged incident projected onto the circuit. Three clusters do most of the damage: the infield chicane and the Turn 7–10 sequence, the braking zone into the final corners, and the run off the banking at Turn 1 — exactly the places the drivers complained about on air.

Stationary time at the mandatory stop against the quickest of them (Mike Zocher, 60.3 s). Lap-time Evolution

Top-10 lap time per lap. Everybody is slow early on a green, greasy surface, everybody spikes once at their own stop, and the second stint is flatter and quicker than the first — on tyres nobody changed, because at Indy a tyre change costs roughly twelve seconds on top of the fuel.

Battle Proximity

Laps spent within 1.5 s of each other, top 12. The dark square is Benjamin Warnow and Christoph Kiesel — 33 of 42 laps nose to tail for the AM win. Behind it: Zocher–Richter (20), Zörlaut–Marius Becker (18), Marius Becker–Frauenknecht (17) and Maurice Becker–Richter (15).

Race Highlights

Round 9 looked from the timing screen like a procession and was nothing of the sort. Yannick Wonnenberg took pole out of a qualifying session in which the top five were covered by 0.154 s — he and Lukas Zörlaut were separated by a single thousandth at one point — led into Turn 1 and led all 42 laps. What made that possible happened directly behind him: Maurice Becker and Dennis Richter spent twenty minutes locked together over second place, Becker trying the same outside move into Turn 1 lap after lap, Richter answering with the better exit every time. Becker eventually got through, Richter took the place straight back, and by the time it finally stuck the leader had a cushion he never had to defend.

The single mandatory stop shuffled almost nothing — only Markus Groß, pitted early on lap 17 for damage after a heavy first-lap hit, converted it into anything, climbing from P15 back to P7. Wonnenberg stayed out a lap longer than everyone around him and came back out still leading, having gained time. The real fight went to the class battle: Ricardo Still led AM for most of the hour and lost it with a late slide and a brush of the wall, handing Benjamin Warnow and Christoph Kiesel a straight duel that ran to the last corner of the last lap — 0.334 s apart at the flag, after 33 of 42 laps within a second and a half of each other. Kiesel finished it on zero incident points, the only clean sheet in the field. And the night’s hard-luck story was Kevin Homburg: bumper torn off at the start, spins, a black flag for the incident limit and a race over after 31 laps.

CAS GT3 WCT · Season 13 · Round 8

Circuit des 24 Heures du Mans

11 August 2026 · 33 starters · ~60-minute timed race (16 laps) · dry, misty, track 22.6 °C · Full race report ↗

2D telemetry replay — the 66-minute race compressed to 90 seconds. The circuit map is the real Circuit des 24 Heures du Mans (13.6 km, 38 corners).

By the Numbers

Race Winner

Yannick Wonnenberg

Pole with his last qualifying lap, the lead from the green flag, and 16 laps out of 16 in front. No lead change all race — his first win of Season 13 in only his fourth start.

⏱️ The Whole Race

0.639 seconds, 16 laps

Dennis Richter ran between 0.5 s and 1.8 s behind Wonnenberg from lap 3 to the flag and never attempted a move. Wonnenberg afterwards: an unspoken agreement between the two Speed Monkeys cars.

Pole & Fastest Lap

3:52.590 / 3:53.686

Wonnenberg found a tow on his final qualifying lap for pole, 0.315 s clear of Mike Zocher. In the race he set a 3:53.686 on lap 8 — and Zocher answered with a 3:53.687. One thousandth over 13.6 km.

🏆 Driver of the Day

Kevin Homburg

#41 (Pro, Mercedes-AMG) takes it on 0.986: no qualifying time at all, 22 places gained from P32 to P10, 22 overtakes and a single incident point. From Markus Groß, Don Utz and Christian Kaschta.

💥 Lap 2

Four cars, three laps

Michael Gasser and Aaron Hatcher were both out by lap 2, Michele Grupe by lap 3, and Mike Zocher spun from P2 to P17. All four retirements were drivers who parked the car and quit after damage.

AM Class Win

Ricardo Still

Class pole and the class win, P11 overall — and he held Elias Schleich off through the whole first stint. GDC went to Markus Heinzl (P17), from Don Utz and Michael Endres.

Cleanest Run

Three zero-incident sheets

The winner, Gregor Micewski and Elias Schleich all finished on 0 incident points. Toughest race: Marcel Holtmann on 11.

Conditions & Attrition

22.6 °C, misty, 29 finishers

A cool, foggy in-sim morning — track 22.6 °C, air 19.6 °C. 33 of 41 registered drivers appeared; 29 saw the flag, and the single-file rolling start went through the chicane without a scratch.

Data Views

Gap to Leader

Two lines on top of each other for the whole hour: Richter never further than 1.8 s from Wonnenberg, never closer than half a second. The single V across laps 7–8 is the mandatory stop — taken by the entire field inside two laps.

Race Pace

Clean-lap distribution, pit and out-laps removed. On a 13.6 km lap the whole classified field fits inside about six seconds — roughly 2.5 % — which is why track position mattered more than pace.

Pit Stops

Each bar is one pit event. The dense block between minute 28 and minute 37 is the mandatory stop on laps 7–8; the isolated early bars are the lap-2 damage repairs of Endres and Gasser, and the late ones are second visits by drivers who had already lost the race.

Forced Repairs & Penalties

This round’s telemetry log carries no incident stream at all, so this chart shows only what it does contain: two forced repairs, Michael Endres at minute 12 and Michael Gasser at minute 22. The incident points in the classification below are the official iRacing figures.

Position Changes

F1-style position track. The flat yellow line at the top is the whole story of the lead; the two long diagonals climbing from the bottom are Kevin Homburg (P32 to P10) and Markus Groß (P22 to P8). The vertical drop on lap 2 is Mike Zocher’s spin.

Net Overtaking

Green = passes made, red = passes lost. Kevin Homburg tops the field at +21 net, from Markus Groß and Benjamin Warnow. Michael Gasser’s deep negative bar is not a bad race — it is the whole field going past his stationary McLaren on lap 2.

Pit-Lane Service Time

Time spent stationary at the mandatory stop, against the quickest of them. The real one-stop field sits in a narrow 54.8–58.3 s band; the baseline is Michael Endres’s 51.0 s, who split his refuelling across two visits. Only Justin Christiansen and Thomas Herbrig lost genuine time here. This is service time, not net strategic gain.

Lap-time Evolution

Top-10 lap time per lap. Everyone is slow on lap 2 (cold tyres, misty morning), everyone spikes on lap 9 — the out-lap after the stop — and from lap 11 the leaders hold a flat line in the low 3:54s on tyres nobody replaced.

Battle Proximity

Laps spent within 1.5 s of another car. Le Mans produces less of this than a short circuit — the long straights stretch the field — but the leading pair and the Still/Schleich AM duel are both clearly visible.

Race Highlights

Round 8 was the first visit of Season 13 to a circuit where a single mistake costs an entire qualifying session. With lap times near four minutes and qualifying split into two 15-minute sessions, most drivers had two or three timed laps to work with — and two of the championship’s quickest men lost theirs. Markus Groß, the Hockenheim winner, crashed on his flying lap and started P22; Kevin Homburg never got a lap counted and lined up P32. Yannick Wonnenberg, back from holiday, found a tow on his very last lap and took pole by 0.315 s.

The race was decided in two laps and then held for fourteen. Wonnenberg led away cleanly from the single-file start; behind him lap 2 removed Michael Gasser and Aaron Hatcher on the spot and spun Mike Zocher from P2 to P17, and lap 3 claimed Michele Grupe. From there Dennis Richter shadowed the leader between half a second and two seconds for the rest of the hour without ever attacking — an unspoken arrangement, Wonnenberg admitted afterwards. The single mandatory stop, taken by everybody on laps 7–8, changed nothing at all. The real racing was behind: Homburg came from last on the road to P10 for Driver of the Day, Groß recovered to P8, Zocher back to P7, and Ricardo Still held Elias Schleich off through the whole first stint to take the AM class win from class pole. Maurice Becker finished a comfortable third with the car everybody had written off for a top-speed circuit.

CAS GT3 WCT · Season 13 · Round 7

Hockenheimring Baden-Württemberg

4 August 2026 · 35 entries · ~60-minute timed race (34 laps) · dry, track 39–45 °C · Full race report ↗

2D telemetry replay — the ~68-minute race compressed to 90 seconds. The circuit map is the real Hockenheim Grand Prix layout (4.574 km, 16 turns).

By the Numbers

Race Winner

Markus Groß

The #81 Ferrari 296 took the lead on lap 10, gave it back only through the pit cycle and won by +2.95 s — his second win of Season 13.

⏱️ The Whole Race

Three cars, five seconds

Groß, Zocher and Zörlaut finished covered by 4.74 s after an hour, and spent almost all of it within a second and a half of each other — touching, in Andreas’s words, at almost every braking point.

Pole & Fastest Lap

1:37.931 / 1:37.982

Mike Zocher took pole by 0.026 s from Zörlaut, with Groß third — the top three inside seven hundredths. Groß then set the race’s fastest lap on lap 11, still shy of the pole time.

🏆 Driver of the Day

Klaus Oberländer

#86 (Pro, BMW M4) takes it on 0.750: up 11 places from P24 to P13, 20 overtakes and just 4 incident points — the cleanest of the big climbers, from Justin Christiansen and Aaron Hatcher.

💥 Lap 1

One yellow, plenty of damage

Contact put Mike Girenz’s Porsche sideways and collected half a dozen cars behind. The full-course yellow ran three laps — the only caution of the race.

AM Class Win

Elias Schleich

P9 overall and the class lead taken from Warnow in the pit phase — the first win for his new team simracelab.de, from Benjamin Warnow (P11) and Ricardo Still (P12).

Cleanest Run

Robert Gradwohl

The only zero-incident sheet in a 34-car field, on the way to P20 — and the quickest genuine service stop of the race at 27.8 s.

Conditions & Attrition

45 °C, no retirements

Track temperature peaked past 45 °C; with a 12-second surcharge for a tyre change nobody took new rubber. Despite that, all 34 starters made the flag.

Data Views

Gap to Leader

Three lines sit on top of each other from lap 5 to the flag. The staggered V between laps 16 and 27 is the mandatory pit window; the field-wide compression at laps 2–4 is the full-course yellow.

Race Pace

Clean-lap distribution, pit and caution laps removed. The leading group stacks into the low 1:39s on a track hot enough to cook the tyres inside two laps.

Pit Stops

Each orange bar is one pit event. The mandatory stops spread from minute 31 to minute 47 because the three yellow laps saved everyone fuel; the isolated bars near minute 5 are lap-1 damage repairs, and Mario Severn’s 66 s is the closed-pit-lane penalty.

Incident Timeline

103 logged telemetry events by driver and race time — 84 off-tracks, 15 collisions, 4 spins. The dense column on the far left is the opening-lap chain reaction. Incident points in the table are the official iRacing figures, which are higher.

Position Changes

F1-style position track. The flat top three tell the story of the race; the long diagonal climbing from the bottom is Maurice Becker, from last on lap 1 to P10.

Net Overtaking

Green = passes made, red = passes lost. Aaron Hatcher tops the field on +18 net. Becker made just as many passes (35) but was passed 39 times while he was going backwards on lap 1, which is why his bar reads negative.

Incidents on Track

Every logged incident projected onto the circuit. The two hot spots are exactly where Andreas predicted them: the exit of the Nordkurve, and the Spitzkehre at the end of the Parabolika.

Pit-Lane Service Time

Stationary time at the mandatory stop versus the quickest of them (Gradwohl, 27.8 s). The whole leading group is inside three seconds of the best, which is why nothing changed hands in the pit phase. This is service time, not net strategic gain.

Lap-time Evolution

Top-10 lap time per lap across the 34 laps. The plateau at laps 2–4 is the yellow; after it, the leaders hold a remarkably flat line on a set of tyres nobody was going to replace.

Battle Proximity

Laps spent within 1.5 s of another car. Hockenheim produced far more of this than expected — the lead trio never separated, and the midfield ran in nose-to-tail packs for the full hour.

Race Highlights

Round 7 delivered the closest front-running battle of Season 13 on a track its own commentator openly dislikes. Mike Zocher took pole by 0.026 s and produced the start of the night — 1.5 seconds clear inside a lap — but behind him the opening lap folded in on itself: contact spun Mike Girenz’s Porsche across the circuit, half a dozen cars were caught, and the race’s only full-course yellow ran for three laps. It cost Maurice Becker everything, dropping the P6 qualifier to last, and it produced the oddest penalty of the season when Mario Severn pitted into a closed pit lane on lap 2, sat for 66 seconds and was ordered to give the entire field back.

Markus Groß took the lead on lap 10 and set the fastest lap on lap 11, but never escaped: Zocher and Lukas Zörlaut stayed inside a second and a half of him for the next fifteen laps, and the pit phase — 28.8 s for Groß and Zörlaut, 29.8 s for Zocher — changed nothing at all. They finished 2.95 s and 4.74 s apart. Elias Schleich took the AM win in P9 for his new team simracelab.de, Björn Krumpschmied won the Gentleman Driver class, and Klaus Oberländer — P24 to P13 on four incident points — took Driver of the Day. Maurice Becker was the other recovery story, swept to last on lap 1 and back to P10 with 35 overtakes. Remarkably for a race this rough, every one of the 34 starters was still running at the flag.
