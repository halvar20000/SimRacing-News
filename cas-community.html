🏃 About the CAS Community

Where passion meets technology — a home for iRacing enthusiasts

Community

Welcome to CAS

The CAS-iRacing Community is a welcoming place for sim racers of all skill levels. Whether you're a newcomer just getting started in iRacing or an experienced racer, CAS offers a supportive environment to improve your driving and connect with like-minded enthusiasts.

cas-community.com
Philosophy

Inclusive & Respectful Racing

CAS values an open, non-elitist atmosphere. The community focuses on mutual support, fair racing, and helping weaker drivers improve. Aggressive and disrespectful behavior is not tolerated — clean racing and sportsmanship come first.

Core Values
Founder

Founded by Andreas Wuschnakowski

Active in sim racing since 2021, Andreas founded CAS out of a desire to create a better community experience for newcomers. His own early experiences inspired the community's welcoming and supportive approach.

🏁 Leagues & Series

Well-organized leagues and individual events — close to real motorsport

League

GT3 – WCT (World Championship Tour)

The flagship GT3 series of the CAS community. Race the world's best GT3 machinery in a professionally organized championship that aims to replicate the excitement of real-world GT3 racing.

Details
League

IEC – International Endurance Championship

Endurance racing at its finest. Team up with fellow community members for longer races that test consistency, strategy, and driver management. A true test of teamwork and patience.

New for next season: The IEC will use iRaceControl for professional-grade race management — live tracking, automated stewarding, incident logging, and PDF race reports.

Scoring rule: Teams must complete at least 90% of the race distance to be eligible for championship points. Teams that finish below the 90% threshold are classified but do not score.

Details
League

Combined Cup Paused

A rotating single-class format — each week a completely different car. The current season cycles through three totally different machines: the Radical, the Ray FF1600 and a TCR touring car. That mix of philosophies — high-downforce prototype, lightweight open-wheeler, and front-drive tin-top — makes the Combined Cup one of the most demanding series in the community: braking points, balance and driving style must be re-learned week after week. Not a multi-class race.

Season paused: After Round 4, the 10th season of the Combined Cup has been paused due to insufficient participation from registered members. The R1–R4 results remain on record below. A return in a future season is possible — watch the CAS community channels for updates.

📝 Latest Race Report

CAS GT3 WCT Season 12 · Round 9 · Suzuka GP · 19 May 2026 · Maurice Becker’s monsoon hat-trick

🏁 New: watch this race back as an animated 2D replay and browse the full telemetry classification in the CAS Race Center. Open Race Center →

🏆 M. Becker, M. Becker, Zocher

🥇 Maurice Becker — M&J Downforce, Porsche 911 GT3 R
🥈 Marius Becker — Speed Monkeys, Ford Mustang GT3
🥉 Mike Zocher — Speed Monkeys, Porsche 911 GT3 R

Pole: Wonnenberg (1:57.691) · 31 starters · Maurice Becker’s 3rd win in a row after Fuji and Brands Hatch · the two Beckers are namesakes, not relatives · fastest lap: Mike Zocher 2:21.669

AM Podium

🏆 Christiansen, Oberländer, Krieger

🥇 Justin Christiansen — NEON Sim Sports Blue, BMW M4 GT3 · overall P9
🥈 Klaus Oberländer — Dan Küchen Motorsport, BMW M4 GT3 · overall P11
🥉 Michael Krieger — Hafenegger Motorsport, Porsche · overall P12

AM pole: Klaus Oberländer (1:58.394) · Christiansen climbed from P15 on the grid to win the class · Krieger recovered from P23

Race Highlight

🌧️ A full-on monsoon

Qualifying ran in the dry, but a wall of rain swept across Suzuka right on cue for the race. Lap times ballooned from the high 1:57s in the dry to the low 2:22s in the wet — spray and fog made Turn 1 almost invisible. Seven cars were classified DSQ after retiring early. Maurice Becker simply drove away from it all, building a lead north of half a minute.

Andreas, mid-race: “One thing I can promise you — this is not going to get any better.”

📋 Full race report

Setting

Suzuka International Racing Course (Japan) — 5.8 km, the famous figure-eight Grand Prix layout. 31 drivers on the grid (of ~50 registered) — the usual summer-season thinning. Early morning in Japan time, 20 °C track at the start. Andreas welcomed back a familiar voice in the booth: co-moderator Philipp Kellin, on the broadcast for the first time in a long while.

The forecast was the story before a wheel had turned: a heavy rain front was tracking straight for the circuit, with a near-100% chance of rain pegged for almost exactly race start.

Qualifying — in the dry

Two 15-minute open sessions, AM then Pro, run before the rain arrived. Yannick Wonnenberg took pole with a 1:57.691 in the Lamborghini — but the front of the grid was desperately tight: Maurice Becker P2 (1:57.733), Mike Zocher P3 (1:57.755), Mario Severn P4 (1:58.030), Lukas Zörlaut P5, Marius Becker P6. The top six were covered by under seven tenths.

In AM, Klaus Oberländer (Dan Küchen, BMW) took class pole with a 1:58.394, good enough for P7 on the combined grid. Kevin Osiewacz (Dat muss Kesseln) qualified P8, Hendrik Stanzel P9, Gregor Micewski P10. Championship leader Yannick and second-placed Gregor had both completed every round so far.

The rain arrives

The radar “green wall” Andreas had been watching all session turned out to be the leading edge of something far heavier. By the warm-up the front of the lap at Spoon was already wet; by the green flag it was a full wet race. Whatever strategy debate there had been about slicks vs. wets evaporated — everyone needed rain tyres. Lap times dropped from the dry 1:57s into the 2:22 range, and visibility — not grip — became the limiting factor for the full hour.

Race — the front

Maurice Becker made the most of the conditions immediately. The Porsche has been the rain car all season, and in the spray he was untouchable — he moved into the lead in the opening laps and just kept extending, the gap reaching as much as 80 seconds at one point before he eased off to bring it home over half a minute clear. His third straight win after Fuji and Brands Hatch.

Behind him the standout drive belonged to Marius Becker (no relation) — the lone Speed Monkeys Mustang, normally a midfield runner, threaded the Mustang through the monsoon from P6 on the grid to a brilliant P2. Mike Zocher completed an all-Speed-Monkeys top three in P3 and set the fastest lap of the race, a 2:21.669. Pole-sitter Yannick Wonnenberg had a far rougher afternoon — repeated offs at Turn 1, where the spray hid the corner entry entirely, and a black flag along the way — but salvaged P4.

It was a monster team result for the Speed Monkeys: four cars in the top seven (Marius Becker P2, Zocher P3, Wonnenberg P4, Dennis Ulli Richter P7).

Drive of the day — Dennis Ulli Richter

“Captain Monkey” Dennis Ulli Richter had a miserable qualifying — he never set a clean lap and started P20, openly stating he hates the circuit. In the race he was a different driver: a calm, mistake-free run through the chaos brought him all the way back to P7, the kind of points haul that matters once the three drop-scores are calculated at season’s end. Mike Girenz produced a similar climb, FRAMIDI Racing’s Porsche coming from P16 to P5.

AM class

Justin Christiansen (NEON Sim Sports Blue, BMW M4) drove the AM race of the day — up from P15 on the grid to P9 overall and the AM class win. Klaus Oberländer, the AM pole-sitter, brought his Dan Küchen BMW home P11 for AM P2; Michael Krieger recovered from P23 on the grid to P12 and the final AM podium spot. Last round’s Adelaide AM winner, Manfred Baar, was an early casualty of the rain.

Attrition — seven DSQ

The rain took a heavy toll. Seven cars retired early enough to be classified DSQ: Antonio Cursio (15 laps), Christian Schlosser (11), Djavit Segashi (9), Alexander Thieme (7), Benjamin Schlosser (6), Manfred Baar (4) and Dirk Bolte (0).

Thomas Herbrig (CAS Tech Performance, McLaren) did race this time — but lost his rear wing in a Turn 1 crash and ran much of the race without it, picking up a black flag before finishing P25. A handful of others, including the two Beckers’ midfield rivals, spent the hour ping-ponging between the walls.

Final classification — top 12

P1 Maurice Becker · P2 Marius Becker · P3 Mike Zocher · P4 Yannick Wonnenberg · P5 Mike Girenz · P6 Lukas Zörlaut · P7 Dennis Ulli Richter · P8 Gregor Micewski · P9 Justin Christiansen (AM win) · P10 Hendrik Stanzel · P11 Klaus Oberländer (AM P2) · P12 Michael Krieger (AM P3).

Off-track notes

  • Maurice Becker’s hat-trick — three wins in a row (Fuji, Brands Hatch, Suzuka) turns the title race sharply his way. Yannick Wonnenberg led the standings going into Suzuka with Gregor Micewski second; with three drop-scores in play and three rounds left, the run-in is wide open.
  • The broadcast itself struggled — the rain rendering load plus iRaceControl overwhelmed Andreas’s streaming PC, causing visible lag and dropped camera feeds. “I might have to take 10,000 euro out of the team kitty and buy an upgrade,” he joked.
  • No post-race interviews — after an hour in the monsoon, the drivers had had enough and the interview room stayed empty. Entirely understandable.
  • Seven no-RSVP / no-show penalty points went to absent drivers: Florian Eigner, Leon Klein, Wohl Joe, Julian Borowski, Carl Sören Schober, Ralph Mielke, Luca-Maximilian Stein.
  • Next round: Magny-Cours GP (France), 26 May 2026. The season then closes with the Special Event finale — the track is going to a community vote from a shortlist of five unusual circuits.

Narrative based on the cas_sim_tv broadcast by Andreas and co-moderator Philipp Kellin. Qualifying times, race results, fastest lap and DSQ flags verified against the authoritative CAS League Scoring (CLS) export.

🎤 Post-Race Briefing — Brands Hatch R7

The Speed Monkeys’ Wednesday community debrief — this week dominated by Brands Hatch’s first live trial of the automatic Full-Course Yellow system

Full-Course Yellow Trial

30 incidents in lap 1 felt too low

For the first time, the league’s automated FCY system triggered live: the 30-incident threshold inside lap 1 fired after the opening accordion-effect contacts, putting the field under a 3-lap caution. The room’s read: too aggressive a reaction for what was mostly minor wing damage and rapidly self-correcting spins. Proposed retune: ~40 incidents within a 15-second window, race-long (not just lap 1), with the final 5 minutes excluded so nobody can game the end of the race.

Damaged-Car Etiquette

Audi without a front splitter, Porsche caught in the chain

An early-race contact ripped the front splitter off Benjamin Warnow’s Audi (the only Audi on the grid). Driving on without it produced massive understeer and unpredictable lines — eventually a Porsche tried to pass, was tagged off-line by the recovering Audi, and Carl Sören Schober’s Mercedes following inherited the spinning Porsche through no fault of his own. Reminder of regulation 2.6: a damaged car must stay off the racing line, and pit if not safely controllable.

Dive-Bomb Verdicts

Two attempts that the room sided against

Two early overtakes got the longest analysis. A Mustang dove a BMW into Turn 1 — physically alongside but not enough alongside per league rules; verdict was “Mustang should have lifted.” And Antonio Cursio dove past Justin Christiansen’s very-early-braking line into the blind right-hander — a contact several drivers admitted they’d been tempted to attempt themselves. Verdict: not malicious, but the wrong corner to overtake in.

📋 Full briefing notes

The big debate: tuning Full-Course Yellow

This was the longest discussion of the night. The current rule is “30 incident points in lap 1 triggers a 3-lap FCY.” Brands Hatch saw it fire for the first time. Most of the room felt the threshold was hit by mild accordion-effect rear-enders — cars that, by sheer luck, all stayed pointing the right way. The aftermath felt heavier than the cause.

Counter-argument from the room: with one bad bounce a low-speed train could just as easily turn into a half-field crash like Spa, where an FCY would have saved the race for many. The system reads numbers, not severity.

Where the room landed:

  • Move from lap-1-only to a rolling time window — e.g. 40 incident points within 15 seconds — so a real mid-race pile-up also triggers FCY.
  • Bump the threshold up to ~40 (vs. 30) to filter out pure accordion noise.
  • Exclude the last 5 minutes so nobody can deliberately cause a pile-up to freeze positions at the end.
  • Keep automation rather than putting the call on a human; consistency beats subjective judgement, and the operator should be free to focus on the broadcast.

Lap 1 / Turn 1–2 — Audi front splitter and the chain reaction

An early-race contact in the opening sequence ripped the front splitter clean off Benjamin Warnow’s Audi R8 (the field’s only Audi). Antonio Cursio’s McLaren was also part of the same scrap and luckily got straightened out without losing the lap. The Audi continued with massive understeer, sliding into the corners line-after-line and unable to hold a predictable trajectory. A pattern observed across multiple recent races: the GT3 cars now lose front bodywork on contact that would have been cosmetic two seasons ago, and a damaged car driving the racing line becomes a moving hazard.

The Audi–Porsche–Mercedes chain — and Reg 2.6

Several laps later the damaged Audi was being overtaken when its erratic lines clipped the passing Porsche at the corner exit. The Porsche spun, and Carl Sören Schober’s Mercedes — right behind, trying to thread past both — had nowhere to go. Schober was the day’s clearest unfortunate victim of someone else’s incident.

The room agreed the relevant rule is league regulation 2.6: “If a driver is significantly slower than other cars due to damage, the car should be driven outside the racing line. If the car is no longer safely controllable, the driver must pit or use the Escape function.” The complication: the in-cockpit damage indicator doesn’t distinguish between “some aero loss” and “your splitter is gone”, and many drivers — including Warnow afterwards — admitted they didn’t realise the full extent of the damage from inside the car. Lesson: if you’re visibly off-pace and unpredictable, treat that as the trigger to stay off-line, regardless of what the indicator says.

Turn 1 dive: Mustang vs. BMW

Early-race overtake attempt: a Mustang dove down the inside of a BMW into the first braking zone. On the replay the Mustang was physically alongside — but not the “side-by-side enough” threshold the league’s current rules require, where the front of the inside car needs to be at least at the rear axle of the outside car at the apex. By the apex the overlap was actually shrinking, suggesting the inside car was already easing out of it.

Discussion split between “the BMW was open and should have anticipated the move” and “at this geometry the inner car wasn’t alongside enough.” Most of the room landed on the latter — with the caveat that the BMW could have closed the inside line earlier to remove the ambiguity. No malice; both could have done a bit better.

Antonio Cursio × Justin Christiansen — the blind right

Antonio Cursio dove down the inside of Justin Christiansen at a blind right-hander where Justin had been braking unusually early all race. Multiple drivers in the room volunteered that they’d been tempted to attempt the same overtake themselves but had thought better of it. Verdict: it’s genuinely not an overtaking corner; the early-braking pattern was the bait. Antonio took the bait. Damage but no race-ending consequence.

Bernhard Wlach’s lapped-car puzzle

Bernhard Wlach (Porsche) had a confusing FCY: his crew chief told him to line up behind car #46 (Max Coldron), but Coldron was flashing him to come past — the system was supposed to allow lapped cars to un-lap themselves (“Lucky Dog”), but it didn’t engage for Wlach because he was several laps down, not just one. The end result: Bernhard had to let an entire train of cars through under blue flag. Settled now, but worth flagging that the un-lap mechanic only applies to single-lap-down cars.

Lap 5/6 chain-reaction recoveries

Mustang #83 had a contact, spun, kissed a kerb, recovered, and then re-spun on the very next corner. Pattern noted: cold or dirty tyres after a recovery + the instinctive “get going fast” reaction = throttle on a dirty contact patch = second spin. The takeaway agreed in the room: after a recovery, take an extra two seconds. Wait until you’re fully on tarmac, the wheel is straight, and the tyre is rolling, before squeezing the throttle.

FCY etiquette — staggered lines, not slalom

Drivers further back in the field complained about getting bunched and brake-checked during the FCY laps because of front-runners weaving and varying speed to keep tyres warm. The room agreed:

  • Drive a constant speed during FCY. The pace car target is around 95 km/h; sustained 160 km/h chases at the front cause the rear of the field to crunch.
  • Stagger left/right in straight lines so you can see past the car ahead — don’t slalom.
  • Warm tyres by gently trailing the brake at low percentage (say 5 %); that warms the rim through the disc continuously, with no fuel cost. Don’t do brake-then-throttle pulses — that just bunches the field.

FCY pit-lane mechanics

For the strategists: when FCY is active, the pit lane is closed in the first lap of caution and reopens for one further lap. If you arrive when it’s closed, two options:

  • Stop just before the entry, wait for it to reopen, drive in clean. No penalty, but you may lose a lap if it doesn’t open this cycle.
  • Drive in anyway, take the ~40-second stationary penalty, do your stop. Often still a smaller loss than running out of fuel or being held outside on damage.

Take-away for race strategy: keep one extra lap of fuel as buffer if you can — an FCY at the wrong moment can cost a clean stop otherwise.

Blue-flag automation — positive update

Refreshing change of tone vs. last week: the automated blue-flag whisper system worked cleanly at Brands Hatch. No penalties given, no complaints in the room about being held up unfairly, and several drivers actively said they thought the lapping flow was the smoothest it had been all season. Keep as-is for now.

Summary of the Wednesday post-race briefing hosted by the Speed Monkeys team (Yannick Wonnenberg & Dennis Ulli Richter). Names verified against the iRLeagueManager standings; some incidents may still be under review.

🏁 PCCD Round 5 — Barcelona-Catalunya GP

Race report from Wednesday’s Porsche Cup round on the Catalunya GP variant — 9 drivers, fading daylight, two contrasting 25-minute races

Race 1 Podium

🏆 Rajkovic, Brand, Pfennig

🥇 André Rajkovic
🥈 Willi Brand
🥉 Sean Pfennig

Pole: Willi Brand 1:45.2 · Decided by a slipstream pass on the start-finish straight · Klaus Oberländer out of the race for unknown reasons

Race 2 Podium

🏆 Schlosser, Brand, Rajkovic

🥇 Benjamin Schlosser
🥈 Willi Brand
🥉 André Rajkovic

Reverse-grid start · Schlosser converted pole into his first PCCD win · Rajkovic charged from P8 to P3 after a Lap 1 spin

Championship

🏅️ Silvio absent — Rajkovic closes

With championship leader Silvio Foth not on the grid, André Rajkovic banks a win and a podium to chip into the gap. Willi Brand quietly bagged P2 in both races and the Race 1 fastest lap. The night’s headline performance: Rajkovic’s recovery in Race 2 from a Lap 1 Turn 1 spin to the bottom step of the podium.

Fastest lap R1: Willi Brand, 1:45.07 · Fastest lap R2: Benjamin Schlosser, 1:45.45 · Next round: Silverstone GP, Wednesday 21 May

📋 Full race report

Setting

Circuit de Barcelona-Catalunya — 4.65 km, 16 corners, the GP variant with the tight final chicane. A long-time Formula 1 test venue, famously rewarding aero balance and tyre warm-up. Track temperature 21°C, fading daylight as the night went on — cars running with headlights for the closing stints. Nine drivers on the grid. Championship leader Silvio Foth absent, Olli also a late scratch.

Qualifying

6-minute session, 3 timed laps. Times tumbled across the session as tyres came up to temperature. Willi Brand stunned the booth with a 1:45.2 to take pole — the broadcaster called it “sensational.” Sean Pfennig matched a 1:45 to slot into P2; Rajkovic, normally the man to beat, found himself only P3 with a 1:45.4. Starting grid: Brand, Pfennig, Rajkovic, Oberländer, Schlosser, Felix, Cavoto, Mielke, Schrader.

Notable: Ralph Mielke joined the field as the third CAS Tech Performance car (Olli having cancelled), setting a 1:47.8 to qualify P8.

Race 1 — 25 minutes, standard grid

An exceptionally clean opening — nine cars through Turn 1 unscathed and not a single incident report flagged for the first ten minutes. Brand led from pole, Rajkovic glued himself to the gearbox for a couple of laps and then took the lead with a textbook slipstream pass on the start-finish straight after a perfect exit out of the final chicane.

Behind them Pfennig settled into a lonely P3, and the second pack — Mielke, Cavoto, Schrader, Felix, Schlosser — traded position over the long lap. Thomas Felix produced one of the moves of the race, a sweeping outside line on Schrader at the final chicane — only to give it back the next lap when Schrader returned the favour.

The night’s only real attrition came from Klaus Oberländer, who pulled into the pits and parked the car for reasons that never made it onto the broadcast — the booth’s incident overlay didn’t flag it either. Possibly a technical problem, possibly something else. Benjamin Schlosser also had a moment somewhere in the pack that the broadcast’s incident overlay missed; he dropped well off the back.

Race 1 result: Rajkovic, Brand, Pfennig, Mielke, Cavoto, Schrader, Felix, Schlosser, Oberländer (DNF). Fastest lap: Willi Brand 1:45.07.

Race 2 — 25 minutes, reverse-grid top 8

The reverse grid put Schlosser on pole and Rajkovic back in P8. Lights out and the script tore itself up at Turn 1 — Rajkovic was tagged into a half-spin and dropped to last. The booth’s read: “a touch, almost certainly unintentional.” A second small contact further back disrupted the chasing pack as well.

Schlosser used the chaos to build a 1.6-second cushion in clean air. The race’s defining storyline became Rajkovic’s charge: through the field, into the points, then onto the podium fight inside ten minutes — up to P2 by the halfway mark, with Brand keeping pace just behind. The broadcaster’s line: “unbelievable performance, no frustration, just heads down and back through them.”

Up front Schlosser kept his discipline. With Brand and Rajkovic engaged in a private duel over P2, Schlosser’s lap times stabilised at exactly the level he needed and the gap stretched back out to the two seconds he’d need to coast home. Behind, Klaus Oberländer spun on his own and dropped places he’d been recovering all race; Jan Tobias Schrader had a costly spin too. Felix and Cavoto traded positions repeatedly in the second-half scrap.

On the very last lap, Brand finally found a way through on Rajkovic for P2 — but the win was already gone. Benjamin Schlosser took his first PCCD race victory with the additional reward of fastest lap (1:45.45). Rajkovic, despite the spin, still walked away with P3.

Race 2 result: Schlosser, Brand, Rajkovic, Pfennig, Felix, Schrader, Oberländer, Cavoto. (Mielke did not take the restart.) Fastest lap: Benjamin Schlosser 1:45.45.

Notes from the booth

  • Two CAS Tech Performance Porsches on track tonight: Willi Brand (P2/P2) and Ralph Mielke (P4 in R1, did not start R2). Olli cancelled.
  • Without championship leader Silvio Foth, the night turned into a fight between Rajkovic, Brand and Schlosser — and the points table tightens slightly as a result, though the gap to Silvio remains substantial with three rounds to go.
  • Klaus Oberländer retired from Race 1 with no public explanation — the broadcast’s incident overlay missed it and the cause never came up on stream. He returned for Race 2.
  • The broadcast’s in-house Race Manager (built by Thomas Herbrig with Claude) missed a couple of flags tonight — sensitivity dial needs another pass.
  • Next round: Silverstone GP, Wednesday 21 May 2026. Then Hockenheim GP and Okayama (Full Course) to close the season.
